# ADD-ON — JAPANESE ROADMAP

## QUICK START

My ladder:

**Kana → basic grammar → N5 → N4 → N3 → N2 → N1/business Japanese → real workplace Japanese**

JLPT is useful, but it is not the whole language.

The official JLPT site describes five levels, N5 through N1. The exam measures language knowledge, reading and listening abilities through its test sections; it should therefore be treated as one measurement of Japanese ability, not a complete test of speaking/writing.

Official site:

https://www.jlpt.jp/

## BANGLADESH

20–30 minutes/day when possible.

Focus:

- kana
- grammar
- vocabulary
- kanji in context
- listening
- graded reading

Free-first resources:

- IRODORI: https://www.irodori.jpf.go.jp/
- Anki: https://apps.ankiweb.net/
- Tadoku: https://tadoku.org/japanese/
- NHK Easy Japanese/news resources
- Comprehensible Japanese
- Tae Kim grammar guide

Do not use ten resources at once.

## EUROPE

Increase Japanese gradually.

Year 1: N4 trajectory

Year 2: N3 trajectory

Year 3: N2 trajectory

These are targets, not guaranteed schedules.

## BEFORE JAPAN

Shift from exam-only study toward:

- workplace vocabulary
- polite speech
- technical terminology
- meetings
- email/chat
- reading documentation
- explaining incidents

## IN JAPAN

Use Japanese every day:

- workplace conversations
- forms
- transport
- shops
- news
- technical documents
- social interaction

Keep formal study because immersion alone can leave gaps.

## GATE

For each level, I should combine:

1. grammar
2. vocabulary
3. kanji
4. reading
5. listening
6. timed JLPT practice
7. real-world comprehension

Passing an exam is evidence. It is not the finish line.

# 11 — AFTER JAPAN PR

## QUICK START

PR is not the finish line.

Once I have permanent residence, the goal changes from **immigration optimization** to **building a good long-term life**.

### Priorities

1. Career quality.
2. Financial independence.
3. Stable housing.
4. Family planning.
5. Health and daily life.
6. Continued Japanese.
7. International optionality.
8. Personal projects and interests.

## CAREER

I can become more selective about:

- technical direction
- employer
- management vs individual contributor path
- remote/hybrid work
- platform/SRE/cloud specialization
- architecture
- AI systems integration

I should not stop learning. I should stop learning purely for immigration points.

## MONEY

Targets should shift toward:

- emergency fund
- retirement/investment planning
- housing decisions
- family support
- education
- long-term savings

Use current Japanese tax/investment rules rather than assuming today's rules will remain unchanged.

## FAMILY

If I eventually support or reunite with family, treat immigration rules for each family member as a separate research problem.

Do not assume my PR automatically gives relatives the same status.

## JAPANESE

Keep improving because it is now part of life, not just a migration tool.

Aim for:

- natural workplace communication
- paperwork
- friendships
- news
- technical discussions
- deeper cultural understanding

## CAREER EXPANSION

Possible later directions:

- Senior Cloud/SRE
- Platform Architect
- Staff Engineer
- Engineering leadership
- AI infrastructure
- MLOps
- distributed systems
- independent consulting

The choice should follow actual strengths and opportunities.

## LIFE RULE

I wanted Japan PR because it was supposed to create stability and freedom.

Once I have that stability, I should use it to build a life, not turn PR into another endless checklist.

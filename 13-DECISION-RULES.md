# 13 — DECISION RULES

## QUICK START

When I am confused about what to do next, I come here before adding another course, language, certification or technology.

## 1. ONE MAIN TECHNICAL SKILL

At any moment, one skill gets the main learning block.

Everything else is maintenance or support.

## 2. GATES BEFORE PROGRESSION

I move on after I can:

1. explain it
2. practice it
3. build with it
4. debug it
5. document it
6. repeat it

## 3. ONE COURSE + DOCS + PROJECT

Ten courses do not create ten times the skill.

My default is:

**one primary learning resource + official docs + hands-on project + self-test**

## 4. NEW TECHNOLOGY NEEDS A REASON

I add a tool/language because:

- university needs it
- target jobs repeatedly need it
- the current system genuinely benefits from it
- it fills a clear foundation gap

“Everyone is learning it” is not enough.

## 5. DO NOT CHASE TITLES

A job title can be misleading.

I look at:

- what systems I touch
- what I own
- how much production exposure I get
- how much engineering I actually do
- whether the experience transfers to the next role

## 6. SKILL > PROOF > EXPERIENCE > COMMUNICATION > CERTIFICATE

Certification can help, especially when a job values it, but it does not replace capability.

## 7. PRODUCTION BEATS TOY COMPLEXITY

One system I deploy, observe, intentionally break and repair can teach me more than many tutorial clones.

## 8. AI COMES AFTER SYSTEMS

AI becomes much more useful to me once I understand:

- computers
- Linux
- networking
- databases
- APIs
- containers
- cloud
- security
- deployment
- observability

Then AI systems integration has somewhere solid to live.

## 9. JAPANESE NEVER EATS THE FOUNDATION

While I am in Bangladesh and busy with university preparation, Japanese stays controlled.

As Japan gets closer, Japanese becomes more important.

## 10. ENGLISH IS PART OF THE ENGINEERING STACK

I do not wait for perfect English.

I learn technical English while learning technology.

## 11. FREELANCING IS OPTIONAL

I only sell work I can independently deliver.

Freelancing can produce money, communication practice and proof. It is not the replacement for the European professional-experience goal.

## 12. IMMIGRATION IS A CONSTRAINT, NOT MY CAREER

I build a real engineering career first.

Then I choose immigration routes that fit the career.

## 13. NEVER TRUST OLD IMMIGRATION NUMBERS

For visa, residence, HSP, PR, work-hour, tax and proof-of-funds information:

**check the current official source before acting.**

## 14. DO NOT SACRIFICE YEARS FOR A DATE

The age target matters, but a forced shortcut that creates weak skills or unstable employment can make the entire plan worse.

## 15. PORTABLE HUMAN CAPITAL WINS

The durable stack is:

**relevant degree + engineering fundamentals + production experience + English + Japanese + evidence of results**

That stack should keep Europe, Japan and other future options open.

## 16. MONTHLY REVIEW, NOT DAILY ROADMAP REWRITING

I review the strategy monthly.

I rewrite the long-term plan only when a major assumption changes.

## 17. THE NEXT ACTION SHOULD BE SMALL

A ten-year plan is useful.

Tomorrow's task should fit on one line.

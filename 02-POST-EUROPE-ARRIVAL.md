# 02 — FIRST MONTH IN EUROPE

## QUICK START

My first month is **settling + surviving + learning the system**, not trying to become a DevOps engineer overnight.

### First priorities

1. Immigration/residence registration required by the destination.
2. Housing and address.
3. Bank/payment setup.
4. Phone/internet.
5. University registration.
6. Health/insurance registration as required.
7. Transport.
8. Grocery/cooking routine.
9. Emergency contacts.
10. Rebuild my normal study schedule.

The exact legal/admin checklist depends on the country.

## FIRST 72 HOURS

- Get safely to accommodation.
- Confirm the accommodation and address.
- Secure passport and important documents.
- Get working mobile data/Wi-Fi.
- Learn the nearest grocery, pharmacy, transport stop and emergency route.
- Tell family I arrived safely.
- Avoid unnecessary purchases.

## FIRST TWO WEEKS

Complete the country's required:

- residence/permit steps
- address registration where required
- university enrolment
- student card
- health/insurance steps
- banking/payment setup
- transport card
- campus accounts
- library/LMS access

Save confirmation documents immediately.

## FIRST MONTH: BUILD A ROUTINE

A realistic week should contain:

- university classes/labs
- focused technical study
- English through technical work
- Japanese
- exercise/walking
- cooking/cleaning/laundry
- administration
- social time
- proper sleep

The routine must be boring enough to survive.

## UNIVERSITY

I should learn:

- grading system
- attendance rules
- exam format
- office hours
- academic integrity rules
- plagiarism/citation rules
- student societies
- career centre
- internship office
- international office

Do not discover a critical university rule two days before an exam.

## MONEY

Track actual spending for the first 4–8 weeks.

Separate:

- fixed costs
- food
- transport
- study
- health
- discretionary
- emergency

After I have real data, replace estimates with reality.

## SOCIAL / PROFESSIONAL

I do not need 500 contacts.

I need:

- a few classmates I can study with
- professors/teaching staff I can approach
- technical communities
- career/internship contacts
- at least some local friends

## TECHNICAL RESET

During the first month, do not abandon the existing ladder.

Maintain:

- Linux
- Git
- Python
- networking

Then align projects with university coursework.

## FIRST-SEMESTER GATE

By the end of the first semester I want:

- stable legal/admin situation
- stable housing/routine
- acceptable academic performance
- working budget
- GitHub activity
- one useful technical project
- a clearer picture of local internship opportunities
